CivCentral · identity concepts · round two

Five marks, five different arguments

Each of these makes a different case for what CivCentral is — a centre, a landmark, a name, a pair of coexisting records, a banner. They share a palette and nothing else: different silhouettes, different construction, different behaviour at 16 pixels. None of them is flat, and none of them borrows a pixel of Minecraft.

The centre, held.

The letter is the whole mark. A heavy ring opens to the right to make the C, and the block sitting inside it is the thing the name promises — one place where everything gathers. Read it as Civ closing around Central.

The ring is extruded rather than drawn, so it sits on the page like a struck coin; the block is a true isometric solid with three lit faces, which is where the Minecraft nod lives — a cube, not a texture.

Every civ server has one place you can see from across the map. This is that argument for the word central: a gold block throwing a column of light, drawn as a night tile rather than a symbol on white.

Its depth comes from light — a glow behind the block, a beam that fades as it climbs, a lit top face — instead of bevels or shadow. It is the only one of the five that is happier on a dark ground, which is where this audience lives.

A typographic route: ten letters drawn on a single grid, every corner cut at the same 16-unit chamfer, then extruded so the word reads as placed blocks with one green cut face. Nothing to explain and nothing to license — the alphabet is drawn from scratch, so it nods at voxels without touching Mojang's type.

Civ takes the green and Central takes the ink, which lets the wordmark split into a short form wherever space is tight.

Reads as
The name, at any size, with no icon to learn

Site header, merch, anywhere horizontal
Trade-off
Needs the C tile to fill a square slot

The initials, threaded. Two Cs cross twice — the gold passes in front at the top, the green passes in front at the bottom — so neither ring is ever the one on top. That weave is the site's founding rule drawn as geometry: conflicting accounts coexist, attributed, and nothing gets overwritten.

Emerald and gold also carry the two layers the wiki already runs on, Record and Account. Depth here is honest occlusion rather than shading — one strand simply goes behind the other.

The most heraldic of the five, and the most game-native: banners are a thing players actually make, and a hall of hung standards is what an archive of civilisations looks like. The C is cut out of the cloth rather than printed on it.

The drape is nine gradient stops across the width, so it reads as fabric catching light instead of a flat pennant shape. Gold rod, swallowtail hem, one clean silhouette.

Reads as
A hanging banner bearing a C
Strongest
Event pages, faction pages, print
Trade-off
The most detailed — below 24 px the folds flatten out

If it were my call

Core as the primary, Blockwork as its wordmark. Core is the one that survives every size and every context without special handling, and it says Civ and Central in a single shape. Pair it with the Blockwork letters and you have a full lockup where the icon and the type are visibly cut from the same material.

Beacon for Discord. A round avatar sits on a dark chat list, and Beacon is the only mark here designed to glow in that context rather than tolerate it.

Interlock and Standard are the riskier picks — Interlock because a monogram asks people to learn it, Standard because detail costs you at small sizes. Both are worth a second round if you want the identity to argue for the archive rather than the hub.
